# UI/UX requirements (guided session with the team lead, 2026-10-27)

Compiled from a guided requirements session (layers 1–3; layers 4–5 skipped at the user's request). Every line comes from the user's answers.

## Why the current design fails
- Generic: reads like a PPT slide or template HTML, flat blocks laid out rather than a crafted surface.
- Static: clicks land instantly with no sense of anything happening; "too fast or lifeless".
- Naive: below the baseline of professional software; nothing responds to the cursor.

## The bar
- Premium on first look: the visitor wants to praise the colours, design and motion together.
- References: Stripe, Vercel, WeMakeDevs (its UI and its moving globe), and sites at that level.
- Judge's first impression wins any conflict with the all-day biller's experience.

## Structure
1. Landing page in front of the app (the first thing a judge sees).
2. Mock login, so it feels like a real product with a workspace.
3. The app (worklist, case, appeal, prior-auth check, run live, how it works).

## Motion
- Landing: one relevant, smooth background moment like the WeMakeDevs globe: it means something about the product and "just feels right". Not the MRI machine; 3D optional.
- App: hover and cursor reactions; clicks feel physical (press) and show checking/progress per standard UI/UX practice; content arrives with choreography.
- Ceiling: alive, but never louder than the content and never frustrating or slowing the user.

## Reject on sight
- Static screens.
- AI slop: emoji, stock/typical headline phrasing, too much text, templated card designs, reused colour palettes.

## Evidence rule
A pattern is adopted when it is common across the top reference sites and is confirmed in our own prototype.
